# SJ Carpentry — confirmed business workflow

Revision: September 23, 2026. Authority: Joe's step-by-step decisions in this
conversation. This is a required business contract for A23 and all affected tasks.
Read with [OPERATING_AGENTS.md](OPERATING_AGENTS.md), [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md)
and [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md). These documents specify desired behavior;
they do not assert that automation is implemented or enabled.

## Precedence and key distinctions

This workflow supersedes conflicting sequencing and approval assumptions in the
September 17 plan and older SOPs. The April SOP supplies background where this
conversation did not change it. Its Houzz-specific instructions are historical;
SJC OS is the intended operational system, with QBO retained initially.

- A signed pre-construction agreement starts scope breakdown, site-visit
  preparation and applicable design preparation. Payment receipt is NOT the gate
  for those activities. They do not wait for a site visit.
- Retain the paid pre-construction gate for the actual measured site visit and
  other paid services not explicitly moved forward here. Do not extend that
  payment gate back onto the signature-triggered preparation above.
- Joe's scope-allocation review and approval to SEND a bid package are separate.
  Every external scope/bid package and supplier pricing request needs release
  approval, including revised packages. An internal draft needs no release.
- Mood boards develop poorly defined finished results. Selection boards help
  clients make unresolved choices. Exact products/finishes supplied by the client
  go directly into the working formal estimate, without a redundant board.
- Owner approval to send a proposal, client acceptance, contract signature,
  invoice issuance, payment receipt and physical completion are distinct facts.
- Internal cost evidence may improve continuously. A sent price is committed
  unless explicitly presented as an allowance. Never silently reprice an offer
  already sent or a signed contract because a supplier changes its cost.

## W01 — Lead qualification and rough estimate

Capture the lead and existing correspondence. Check service area, job type, scope,
budget fit, timeline and goals using established qualification rules. Explain the
process and gather photos and approximate measurements; retain the established
human-call preference without pretending a call happened. An optional introductory
visit is relationship-building, not a substitute for paid measured planning.

Agents request missing facts automatically under the routine communication policy,
stop redundant requests when information arrives, and prepare a supported rough
estimate. Joe approves its exact price/scope before sending. Client willingness
to proceed leads to the pre-construction agreement and its invoice under the
established terms. No deposit percentage, pricing formula or new contract-release
authority for pre-construction is invented by this revision.

## W02 — Signature starts preparation

On verified completion of the pre-construction agreement signature, automatically
start one project workflow and prepare the following from the lead information:

1. A preliminary scope register, broken into work packages, trades, potential
   subcontractors, suppliers, required finishes, dependencies and exclusions.
2. A site-visit plan specific to the scope and unanswered questions.
3. A design brief and the appropriate mood-board/selection/direct-product path.
4. The working formal-estimate structure, with known costs and explicit internal
   gaps; missing values must not silently become zero.

Mark site-dependent assumptions as unverified. Break down reasonably identifiable
work now; do not claim concealed conditions or unmeasured quantities are known.
The authenticated signature event is the trigger; Joe does not need to open the
AI panel or request these outputs. A repeated event resumes the same work.

## W03 — Scope allocation and the site visit

Bring Joe a concise scope-allocation review before bid requests are released. He
can retain any work for himself and supply dedicated pricing. Retaining a scope
removes its labor from subcontractor solicitation, not from the project or its
material needs. Split supply and installation explicitly where appropriate.
Record whether Joe's entered price is internal cost or client selling price;
preserve an established field meaning and ask only if it is ambiguous. Do not
apply markup twice or overwrite a dedicated price with model assumptions.

The site-visit plan identifies, by scope:

- What to inspect and which existing conditions or interfaces matter.
- Dimensions required, their locations/units, and photos needed to interpret them.
- Unknowns and assumptions to verify, including fit, access and installation issues.
- Client questions and decisions, and relevant trade/design information to collect.

Joe uploads notes/photos after the visit. The agent extracts source-linked facts,
measurements, preferences, decisions and issues; updates affected scopes, plans,
takeoffs, selections and estimate inputs; and shows a concise change summary.
It checks for missing information rather than marking every checklist item complete.
Conflicting, illegible or ambiguous findings produce targeted clarification.
Preserve Joe's scope allocations and dedicated pricing unless his notes explicitly
change them. New work or changed quantities must not silently expand his fixed price.

Site-verified measurements support plans/drawings and categorized material takeoffs.
Do not solicit precise quantities that lack a basis. Joe may approve a package at
any time, including before the site visit, when its assumptions and limitations
are visible to him and accurately represented in that package. Do not require all
trades or all project stages to be complete before allowing an individual release.

## W04 — Design direction, selections and feedback

Evaluate each room/scope individually; mixed projects can follow different paths.

| Available client direction | Agent action | Release/choice evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Finished result poorly defined | Prepare a mood board using stated preferences and project facts | Joe approves before client presentation; explicit client direction approval starts selections |
| Direction sufficiently defined, products undecided | Prepare suitable selections directly | Joe approves the selection package before presentation; the client's choices feed the estimate |
| Exact product or finish supplied by the client | Add directly to the working formal estimate; research quantities/specifications/pricing | Source-linked client instruction; no selection-board detour or repeat choice required |

Watch owner and client messages, comments and portal responses for feedback on
the current artifact. Apply requested adjustments and retain revision history.
Clarify conflicting or vague feedback; positive comments are not automatically
approval of an entire package. Joe reviews revised client-facing packages before
release. Prior approval never authorizes a materially different board or selection.

When a mood board is approved by the client, prepare selections derived from that
direction automatically. When the client chooses a selection, incorporate it into
the estimate automatically. Track partial choices independently: unselected options
remain open and are not all added to the total. If an approved direction changes,
identify affected choices, costs, packages and commitments rather than restarting
unrelated work. Site notes can revise these artifacts after their initial preparation.

## W05 — Price discovery and supplier knowledge

For a chosen product with missing pricing, identify the exact model, variant,
finish, unit and needed quantity, then look online and in permissioned historical
quotes/purchases. Use source/date, currency, unit basis and tax/freight coverage.
Do not substitute a similar product without making the difference explicit.
If quantities are unknown, use plans/measurements/site notes or request the precise
missing measurement; web pricing cannot supply an unknown physical quantity.

Find suppliers likely to source the product based on supplier type and history.
Joe named **Siweck Lumber** as an existing lumberyard relationship. A lumberyard
is a candidate for lumber, doors, windows, siding and roofing. This is category
reasoning, not proof of a particular brand, inventory, discount or current quote.
Resolve the supplier's actual contact from trusted records before preparing a send.

Maintain three separate evidence levels:

1. Potential source inferred from supplier category.
2. Historical supply/pricing evidence or an owner-confirmed relationship.
3. Current project/product-specific quoted price and terms.

Joe's relationships and special pricing are mostly undocumented. Build the supplier
record incrementally from his corrections and historical/current quotes. Never
invent a standing discount or apply one job's terms universally. Old quotes remain
dated evidence, not current confirmed prices.

Stage a supplier pricing request containing exact products, quantities or explicit
quantity gaps, needed dates and relevant documents. Notify Joe for approval before
sending; this approval requirement overrides broad automatic-information-request
policy. Use supported online pricing in the draft estimate while waiting, marked
internally as provisional and supplier quote pending. Do not ask Joe to do research
the agent can perform through authorized tools.

## W06 — Review and release of scope/bid packages

Prepare bid packages from the current scope, applicable drawings, takeoffs and
selections. All sends require Joe or an explicitly authorized delegate's approval.
This applies to initial, revised and previously reviewed scopes. A site-note update
never constitutes release permission. Nonbinding quote requests, accepting a bid
for the estimate, awarding work, buying materials and paying bills are distinct.

Each ready package produces an approval card in SJC OS and an actionable notification
through configured Telegram now, native app push when available. Include:

- Verified recipient(s) and trade/supplier role.
- Plain-language included work and important exclusions, including Joe's work.
- Key quantities/units, product specifications and attachment revision list.
- Assumptions, missing information and anything that affects bid comparability.
- Changes since the last review, and the exact effect of approving.

Provide Approve and send, Request changes, Hold, and access to the full artifact.
The summary must represent the actual payload, not a separately generated stale
description. Show critical omissions even if they make the package less attractive.
Allow individual release at any time; readiness notifications do not impose a
project-wide batch gate. Any optional batch approval names every recipient and
version and stores separate per-recipient action outcomes.

Notify once per ready revision, update the same decision when needed, and avoid
duplicate alerts across channels. A fresh substantive revision requires fresh
approval. Already sent artifacts stay immutable; stage a correction/revision for
approval and identify downstream impact. Approving in any channel resumes the
same action once. Follow-up requiring a new/revised scope or pricing request also
needs release approval; ordinary factual status chasing within the approved request
may use routine policy without broadening the request.

## W07 — Continuous formal-estimate assembly and client pricing

| Event | Required automatic estimate update |
|---|---|
| Client specifies a product/finish | Add or update its source-linked estimate item immediately; research missing costs/quantities |
| Client chooses from a selection package | Incorporate chosen items; remove superseded options/allowance coverage without duplication |
| Joe approves a sub bid for estimate use | Incorporate the covered scope and current quote version, including exclusions |
| Noncompetitive supplier quote arrives | Replace the corresponding internal online/provisional cost after checking product, quantity and coverage |
| Competing supplier quotes arrive | Prepare an equivalent-scope comparison and approval decision; do not sum or automatically select competing offers |
| Joe supplies dedicated labor/pricing | Apply to the stated scope and basis; preserve the owner input |
| Site notes or quantities change | Recompute supported affected draft items and highlight approval/commitment impact |

Keep a stable project/scope/item identity and quote revision lineage. A quote for
several items is allocated or linked without counting the whole quote repeatedly.
Check tax, delivery, supply-versus-installation, units and overlapping trade scope.
Store both source cost and client selling price. An approved bid incorporated into
the estimate does not award a contract unless the approval explicitly includes it.

Joe can approve sending the estimate before every supplier quote or choice is
final. His internal review must highlight provisional online prices, missing quotes,
assumptions and margin exposure. Client presentation follows his choice:

- **Committed price:** show the approved client price. Research source and pending
  supplier discount stay internal. Once sent, freeze that offered price; later
  supplier costs change internal margin, not the client amount automatically.
- **Allowance:** explicitly label the allowance and what amount/scope it covers.
  If the client chooses above it, prepare a priced change order for Joe's approval
  and then client acceptance/payment under the applicable terms. Do not disguise
  an adjustable allowance as a committed price.

Any revised offer needs fresh owner approval before sending. An accepted contract
changes only through its change-order process. Unsupported costs are never quietly
zero; obtain evidence, an explicit owner price/assumption, or an explicit allowance.
Do not expose internal provisional flags on otherwise committed client line items.

## W08 — Acceptance, agreement and initial invoice

Prepare the complete proposal package from the estimate, SOW, relevant plans,
visuals and preliminary timeline. Joe approves sending the offer. Record the
client's acceptance of that exact offered revision, distinct from Joe's approval.

Client acceptance automatically triggers the construction agreement using the
established template and accepted SOW, and the invoice under the predetermined
milestone payment structure. No further owner release is needed for these exact
derived documents. This is an explicit exception to per-package scope release:
the matching SOW inside this construction agreement is already covered by the
accepted offer. New scope, nonstandard terms or a mismatched revision are exceptions.

Issuance may precede construction-contract signature/payment. Do not mistakenly
require the signed construction agreement to send the invoice that obtains its
initial payment. Retain stable economic identities when the resulting agreement
is signed, so issuance is not duplicated. Preserve the configured retainer/split-
milestone model or per-contract structure; do not invent percentages or additional
milestone invoices that double-bill an existing retainer. A final invoice charges
only the verified remaining balance, not the whole project again.

## W09 — Schedule, material buyout and project cash

While awaiting construction signature/payment, agents may prepare the construction
schedule, coordinate tentative sub availability and check supplier lead times.
Tentative conversations must not confirm a start date or award/order work.

Prepare the full schedule for Joe's approval, accounting for trade dependencies,
durations, inspection requirements from verified project records, material lead
times, delivery and funding. Joe approves before dates are confirmed with the
client or subs. **Construction agreement signed AND initial payment received** are
also required before confirming dates or placing material orders. Purchase approval
remains a separate requirement; schedule approval does not authorize spending.

Build a material buyout schedule backward from each need-on-site date: quote
validity, order deadline, lead time, deposit/balance terms, delivery window and any
reasonable documented delivery buffer. Align it to expected milestone collections
and planned subcontractor/other project obligations. Surface a future shortfall
early enough to change timing or bring a decision to Joe.

Cash guard: do not commit more project spending than funds actually collected for
that project, net of amounts already spent or reserved for outstanding commitments.
Expected milestone collections support forecasts only. Invoices sent, promised
payments and pending ACH are not collected funds. Avoid counting an order and its
subsequent bill twice. Account for processor fees, refunds/returns and unavailable
funds according to reconciled cash records. Unknown cash status blocks the affected
commitment, not unrelated planning work.

Any use of company cash requires explicit approval identifying the project,
amount, purpose and effect. An ordinary purchase tap does not silently authorize
company funding; a combined decision must expressly show both. Reserve available
funds atomically at approved commitment so two simultaneous purchases cannot spend
the same balance. Reconcile payment against its reservation, release only verified
unused amounts, and escalate returns/shortfalls against existing commitments.
This is a planning/control ledger integrated with QBO, not a new accounting system.

## W10 — Field progress, reports and schedule changes

Use the sub portal and authorized messages already received as evidence. On a
subcontractor's completion report, ask for completion photos only if adequate,
relevant photos have not already been supplied. Prepare the report and evidence
for Joe to confirm physical completion; that confirmation triggers the corresponding
milestone invoice under the predetermined structure. A sub's claim or photos alone
do not authorize completion or billing.

Compile a weekly sub report covering work done, current progress, photos, snags
and relevant discoveries. Proactive updates during the week count toward it.
If evidence is sufficient, do not ask for another report or repeat photos. If it
is incomplete, ask precisely for the missing part. Weekly factual client updates
use suitable verified information; internal notes and unresolved urgent issues
remain private until Joe decides how to communicate them.

Agents may adjust internal tasks inside the approved schedule only if there is
no changed promise to client/subs, cost increase or funding gap. Immediately alert
Joe to changes affecting the client or another sub. Explain impacts and prepare
revised dates and messages for approval before making new commitments.

## W11 — Snags, owner decisions and change orders

Every reported snag immediately alerts Joe with source evidence, affected work,
likely cost/schedule/client impact, recommendation, and the specific decision on
whether affected work should continue. The agent may gather facts and prepare
options; it does not independently decide pause/continue or interpret silence as
permission. Record decision pending and the actual reported site status separately.
Do not instruct anyone to continue unsafe work; existing emergency/site-safety
procedures remain applicable and do not depend on a new AI instruction.

After Joe decides, communicate his instructions and update tasks/schedules. If
additional scope is outside the contract, automatically draft the priced change
order with scope, supported costs, timing and payment requirement. Joe approves
before client release. Client signature and the required change-order payment
precede that additional work. Above-allowance selections follow this process too.
Missing prices create research/quote-request work or a targeted owner assumption,
not invented fixed prices. Keep the pending issue visible without repeatedly
chasing a sub for work that is awaiting Joe's decision.

## W12 — Closeout, post-project and learning

Prepare Joe's internal inspection and punch list. Coordinate sub corrections and
collect evidence; Joe confirms corrections before the client walkthrough is
scheduled. After the walkthrough, track client punch items through resolution and
obtain written client sign-off. Do not confuse uploading a photo with accepting
the quality of the work.

Written client sign-off automatically triggers the final invoice, including
approved remaining change-order balances and applying all recorded payments and
credits. No extra owner release. Reconciliation conflicts remain exceptions;
automatic issuance is not permission to guess a balance or duplicate an invoice.

Automatically send applicable warranty/care documents, request a review, and arrange
the post-project check-in under configured timing/terms. Do not invent warranties
or mistake a review request for permission to publish marketing or enroll a client
in a newsletter. A returned problem becomes tracked work and escalates when an
owner decision is needed. Do not mark financial closeout complete merely because
the final invoice was sent; outstanding payment remains tracked.

Compare estimated versus verified actual materials, sub costs and owner hours at
closeout. Update internal cost observations automatically under A15's evidence,
unit, outlier and rollback rules; retain history and incorporate late adjustments.
Markup/profit targets still require approval. Never train the cost book on online
placeholder prices as though they were actual purchases.

## Completion and configuration boundaries

Every W01–W12 step needs a trigger, inputs, action authority, output evidence,
blocked reason/next action and retry/recovery behavior. Implementations must prove
automatic advancement without an AI-panel prompt, including event-driven estimate
updates and resumption after owner/client replies. [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md)
contains required scenarios; [OPERATING_AGENTS.md](OPERATING_AGENTS.md) defines
runtime instructions and evaluations in addition to application code.

Reuse verified existing milestone terms, contract templates, qualification rules,
recipient identities and follow-up cadences. Configure missing values visibly;
do not re-ask settled decisions or silently import obsolete SOP pricing. Exact
report/check-in times, lead times and initial pricing are data/setup items. The
implementation can be built against fixtures while account and policy setup waits.
